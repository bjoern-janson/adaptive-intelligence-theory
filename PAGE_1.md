# Adaptive Intelligence Theory (AIT)

## Core Question

> **How can a system expand its ability to influence the future while preserving the conditions required for continued improvement?**

Adaptive Intelligence Theory (AIT) views intelligence as a property of adaptive dynamics rather than static capability. The central object of study is not performance alone, but the processes by which systems improve their models, actions, and ability to remain corrigible under changing conditions.

---

# The Adaptive Loop

Every adaptive system participates in a continuous cycle:

```text
Reality
   ↓
Observation
   ↓
Representation
   ↓
Compression
   ↓
Prediction / Search
   ↓
Action
   ↓
Reality Change
   ↓
Feedback
   ↺
```

Formally,

$$
R_t
\rightarrow
M_t
\rightarrow
A_t
\rightarrow
E_{t+1}
\rightarrow
R_{t+1}
$$

where:

- $R_t$ — representation of reality
- $M_t$ — internal model and search process
- $A_t$ — action
- $E_t$ — environment

Intelligence is the capacity to improve this adaptive loop over time.

---

# The Possibility Space Principle

Every system induces a space of reachable futures:

$$
\Omega=\{\text{possible future states}\}
$$

Capability expands this space:

$$
\Delta\Omega>0
$$

However, increasing capability expands both opportunity and risk:

- best achievable future ↑
- worst achievable future ↑

Therefore, the objective is not simply to maximize capability.

Instead, intelligence should maximize beneficial possibilities while minimizing harmful ones:

$$
\Delta\Omega_{\text{good}}
>
\Delta\Omega_{\text{bad}}
$$

The goal is directed expansion of the possibility space.

---

# The Alignment Principle

A system remains adaptively stable when its capacity for self-correction grows at least as quickly as its capacity to transform the world.

Let

- $A_t$ = alignment (or correction) capacity
- $C_t$ = capability

The stability condition is

$$
A_t \ge C_t
$$

If instead

$$
C_t>A_t,
$$

the system enters a regime where its ability to produce change exceeds its ability to detect and correct errors.

Capability without proportional correction produces increasing instability.

---

# The Representation Principle

Optimization is fundamentally limited by representation.

A system cannot reliably optimize what it cannot accurately represent.

Higher-quality representations expand the space of controllable futures by improving:

- prediction,
- search,
- abstraction,
- intervention.

Representation quality therefore determines the effective size and structure of the reachable possibility space.

---

# The Adaptive Objective

An intelligent system should recursively improve:

1. its representation of reality,
2. its ability to act,
3. its ability to detect and correct errors.

while preserving:

- contact with reality,
- reliable feedback,
- alignment with beneficial futures.

Recursive improvement without correction eventually diverges.

Correction without improvement eventually stagnates.

Sustained intelligence requires both.

---

# Central Thesis

> Intelligence is not the ability to find answers.

> Intelligence is the ability to improve the processes that discover, evaluate, and create answers while remaining corrigible under changing reality.
